## Introduction {#introduction}

Homer Resources gathers everything published under the Homer brand, which has
carried this author's software for many years. All of it is free, made by a
blind developer for people who work by keyboard and screen reader.

There are three kinds:

- **Collections** are libraries of documents you download.
- **Directories** are reference guides you read on the web.
- **Tools** are Windows apps you install and run.

Each of the three has its own page as well. The section headings below link
to those pages, and the entries within each section link to the individual
resources.

## [Homer Collections](https://JamalMazrui.github.io/HomerCollections/) {#homer-collections}

Each collection gathers material that is already public but hard to read with
a screen reader, and puts it in one place in a form that reads well. Every
file is named for the title of the document it holds; source documents come
as PDF, Word, Excel, or EPUB, and each is also supplied as HTML and plain
text. Every heading below links to a zip archive of the whole collection.

### [A11yAI](https://github.com/JamalMazrui/A11yAI/archive/main.zip) {#c-a11yai}

A11yAI holds about 60 documents on accessibility and artificial
intelligence. Some address the subject itself, such as ethical AI and prompt
engineering; others are readable guides to particular engines, including
ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini, Otter.ai, and Seeing AI.

### [Access ICT](https://github.com/JamalMazrui/AccessICT/archive/main.zip) {#c-accessict}

Access ICT holds more than 100 documents on accessible information and
communication technology. Topics run from accessibility APIs, screen readers,
and assistive technology to design and development, Office and PDF documents,
captions and audio description, regulations and industry guidelines, testing,
online learning, meetings, and travel.

### [App Help Guides](https://github.com/JamalMazrui/AppHelpGuides/archive/main.zip) {#c-apphelpguides}

App Help Guides holds the official help documentation of popular products,
each publisher's own words gathered from its help centre into a single
document per product. Company help sites bury a three-sentence answer inside
a page of banners, accordions, and chat widgets, while one document has one
heading structure and one search, and works offline. Each guide is
comprehensive rather than selective, and says plainly where the material came
from, when, and what was left out.

### [DevDocs](https://github.com/JamalMazrui/DevDocs/archive/main.zip) {#c-devdocs}

DevDocs holds more than 80 tutorials and reference documents for developers,
covering some 34 languages and tools. Among them are C#, C++, Java,
JavaScript, Python, Swift, Kotlin, Lua, SQL and SQLite, HTML and CSS, Git,
Markdown, Pandoc, Node.js, React, Selenium, VS Code, and Windows batch.

## [Homer Directories](https://JamalMazrui.github.io/HomerDirectories/) {#homer-directories}

Each directory gathers information that is real but scattered. The purpose is
to save the reader the search: someone has already visited the sources,
checked the links, and written down what was found. Each is one web page
organized under headings, with entries in alphabetical order, links that
point to a specific page rather than a home page, and appendixes that let you
browse the same material a second way. Nothing is behind a sign-in, and the
Markdown source of each guide can be downloaded and read offline.

### [AudioVault](https://JamalMazrui.github.io/AudioVault/) {#d-audiovault}

AudioVault lists the English-language movies and television shows that have
audio description in the AudioVault collection, each title linking to its
audio download page. Entries give the year, genres, running time, IMDb rating
and page, and a description where known. Appendixes list the movies and shows
again by year and by genre.

### [Blind Authors](https://JamalMazrui.github.io/BlindAuthors/) {#d-authors}

Blind Authors lists 59 writers who are blind or have low vision, with 86
books they wrote on their own, each first published in 2000 or later and
available today as electronic text or audio. Lists like this tend to be short
or out of date, or to mix in books merely about blindness, so this one names
the author, the book, and where to get it in a form you can read.

### [Blind Creators](https://JamalMazrui.github.io/BlindCreators/) {#d-creators}

Blind Creators brings 115 people who are blind or have low vision into one
alphabetical listing, covering everyone who authors books, develops apps, or
makes presentations. It merges the three companion directories, so someone
who both writes and speaks appears once, with every kind of work under a
single entry.

### [Blind Developers](https://JamalMazrui.github.io/BlindDevelopers/) {#d-developers}

Blind Developers lists 32 software developers who are blind or have low
vision, with software each has built, limited to projects still actively
developed in 2020 or later. Aspiring blind programmers are often told the
work is possible without being shown anyone doing it; this answers with
names, projects, and ways to reach the developers listed.

### [Blind Movers](https://JamalMazrui.github.io/BlindMovers/) {#d-movers}

Blind Movers covers travel, recreation, and tourism while blind or low
vision. Travel advice for the general public rarely says which parts of a
trip actually depend on sight, so this guide names the organizations, tours,
and services that plan for blind travelers, and explains what to expect
before you book.

### [Blind Presenters](https://JamalMazrui.github.io/BlindPresenters/) {#d-presenters}

Blind Presenters lists 36 people who are blind or have low vision and who
lead in time-based media: podcasts, talks and keynotes, video, broadcast, and
film, all from 2010 onward. Speakers are otherwise found through conference
programs and podcast feeds that are scattered and hard to search.

### [Tech Training](https://JamalMazrui.github.io/TechTraining/) {#d-techtraining}

Tech Training is a directory of 1304 free video and audio presentations on
technology and accessibility, drawn from 25 sources and sorted into 20 topic
categories. Some people would rather learn from a demonstration, webinar, or
podcast than from written instructions. Every title links straight to the
presentation with no sign-in, and entries give the year, listening time,
presenter, and source.

## [Homer Tools](https://JamalMazrui.github.io/HomerTools/) {#homer-tools}

The apps do different jobs but are built the same way. What they share:

- **64-bit Windows programs**, written in C# or Python, MIT-licensed, with
  full source code on GitHub.
- **Two ways to work.** Most run as a dialog or from the command line, with
  the same options either way, named consistently across apps, so a task done
  by hand can be repeated in a script.
- **One simple installer each**, the same short wizard every time, offered
  here as a direct download.
- **Speech you can count on.** They speak through JAWS and NVDA directly, and
  raise a Windows notification for Narrator and others, so you hear the
  result of a command.
- **Local work.** Each app runs on your computer, not on someone's server.

Each app ships the same documents, in HTML and Markdown:

- **ReadMe** — introduction and quick start.
- **Announce** — what is new in the current release.
- **The app's own guide** — the full user guide, named after the app.
- **Developer** — how the program is built.
- **History** — changes across versions.
- **Hotkeys** — every keystroke in one list.

### 2htm {#t-2htm}

2htm converts Word, Excel, and PowerPoint files to clean HTML using
Microsoft's own APIs, with options for plain text and for stripping images.
Most converters copy how a document looks on paper, while 2htm keeps what
matters to a screen reader: headings, lists, tables, and reading order.

- Project page: <https://github.com/JamalMazrui/2htm>
- Executable installer: <https://github.com/JamalMazrui/2htm/releases/latest/download/2htm_setup.exe>

### bookFido {#t-bookfido}

bookFido builds one catalog of the books you own or have borrowed across five
libraries: Audible, Bookshare, Goodreads, Kindle, and NLS BARD. No website
does this, since each library knows only its own books, so bookFido reads all
five through your own signed-in copy of Microsoft Edge. You get a searchable
catalog, a spreadsheet, and a database, with each book counted once, and
nothing about your accounts leaves your computer.

- Project page: <https://github.com/JamalMazrui/bookFido>
- Executable installer: <https://github.com/JamalMazrui/bookFido/releases/latest/download/bookFido_setup.exe>

### DbDo {#t-dbdo}

DbDo manages databases from the keyboard, opening SQLite, Access, Excel,
dBASE, and delimited text in their native formats. It offers two ways to work
on the same open database at once: ordinary Windows lists you move through
cell by cell, and a dot prompt for typing commands and SQL. So you can query,
edit, relate, and report without a mouse or a glance at a grid.

- Project page: <https://github.com/JamalMazrui/DbDo>
- Executable installer: <https://github.com/JamalMazrui/DbDo/releases/latest/download/DbDo_setup.exe>

### EdSharp {#t-edsharp}

EdSharp is a full featured editor for text and program code, in use since
2007. Other editors can be made to work with a screen reader, but EdSharp
speaks the result of a command on its own, so you get confirmation without
inspecting the screen. It also handles snippets, structured text, word
processing, math, programming, and scripting add-ins.

- Project page: <https://github.com/JamalMazrui/EdSharp>
- Executable installer: <https://github.com/JamalMazrui/EdSharp/releases/latest/download/EdSharp_setup.exe>

### extCheck {#t-extcheck}

extCheck checks Word, Excel, PowerPoint, and Markdown files for accessibility
problems, using an extensible rule set drawn from the Microsoft Office
Accessibility Checker and the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines. Office
checks one file at a time from inside the app, while extCheck can take a
whole folder, so you find missing headings, missing picture descriptions, and
unlabeled tables before you send anything out.

- Project page: <https://github.com/JamalMazrui/extCheck>
- Executable installer: <https://github.com/JamalMazrui/extCheck/releases/latest/download/extCheck_setup.exe>

### FileDir {#t-filedir}

FileDir manages files and folders by typed command rather than by mouse, and
has done so since 2006. Its commands are grouped by what they do: finding,
going to, navigating, querying, tagging, and transferring. Routine file work
takes fewer keystrokes, and the program tells you what happened.

- Project page: <https://github.com/JamalMazrui/FileDir>
- Executable installer: <https://github.com/JamalMazrui/FileDir/releases/latest/download/FileDir_setup.exe>

### HomerScribe {#t-homerscribe}

HomerScribe describes what happens on screen in a video and writes out what
is said in it, returning a copy of the film with description mixed into its
sound, a timed script of the descriptions, and a transcript. Services that do
this charge by the minute and want your video uploaded, while HomerScribe
runs the AI models on your own machine and makes no network request once
installed.

- Project page: <https://github.com/JamalMazrui/HomerScribe>
- Executable installer: <https://github.com/JamalMazrui/HomerScribe/releases/latest/download/HomerScribe_setup.exe>

### HomerView {#t-homerview}

The browser knows far more about a page than reaches the flat copy a screen
reader builds. HomerView is an NVDA add-on that steers its own copy of
Microsoft Edge and asks the browser directly, so you can count headings,
links, and form fields, check the page for accessibility problems, see where
a link really goes, and pull the main article out of the clutter around it.
No artificial intelligence is involved, and no page you read leaves your
computer.

- Project page: <https://github.com/JamalMazrui/HomerView>
- Executable installer: <https://github.com/JamalMazrui/HomerView/releases/latest/download/HomerView_setup.exe>

### urlCheck {#t-urlcheck}

urlCheck opens web pages in Microsoft Edge and runs the axe-core testing
engine on each, producing a report per page plus a draft Accessibility
Conformance Report covering all 86 WCAG 2.2 success criteria, with a
companion sheet of manual tests. Free web checkers test one page at a time
and keep the results on their servers, while urlCheck works through a whole
list on its own and leaves every file on your disk, ready to send to a
developer or compare against a later scan.

- Project page: <https://github.com/JamalMazrui/urlCheck>
- Executable installer: <https://github.com/JamalMazrui/urlCheck/releases/latest/download/urlCheck_setup.exe>

### urlFido {#t-urlfido}

urlFido downloads files of the kinds you choose from a web page, so a page
offering forty PDF files becomes one command instead of forty trips through a
link list. Ordinary downloaders visit a page as a stranger, while urlFido
drives your own copy of Microsoft Edge, so scripts run, sign-ins apply, and
files a server will release only to a real click still arrive.

- Project page: <https://github.com/JamalMazrui/urlFido>
- Executable installer: <https://github.com/JamalMazrui/urlFido/releases/latest/download/urlFido_setup.exe>
