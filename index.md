## Introduction {#introduction}

Homer Resources gathers everything published under the Homer brand: free
software and reference material by a blind developer, for people who work by
keyboard and screen reader.

There are four kinds: collections of documents you download, directories you
read on the web, tools you install, and the development kit the tools are
built on. Each kind has its own section below, and each section names the web
page that holds the whole set.

## Homer Collections {#homer-collections}

Each collection puts public material that is hard to read with a screen
reader somewhere readable. Source documents come with HTML and plain text
copies, and each entry below links to the collection's own zip archive, which
holds the whole set.

The web page for all of them is [Homer Collections][p-collections].

### A11yAI {#c-a11yai}

About 60 documents on accessibility and artificial intelligence, including
readable guides to ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini, and other AI engines.
[Download][c-a11yai-zip].

### Access ICT {#c-accessict}

More than 100 documents on accessible information and communication
technology, covering assistive technology, design, documents, captions,
regulations, testing, and many other topics.
[Download][c-accessict-zip].

### App Help Guides {#c-apphelpguides}

Publishers' own help documentation for 63 products in 12 categories,
gathered into one document each. A help site buries a short answer under
banners and widgets, while one document has a single heading structure, a
single search, and works offline.
[Download][c-apphelpguides-zip].

### DevDocs {#c-devdocs}

More than 80 tutorials and references for developers, covering Python,
JavaScript, C#, Git, SQL, and many other languages and tools.
[Download][c-devdocs-zip].

### Podcast Directories {#c-podcastdirectories}

Every public episode of 89 podcasts, more than 30,800 in all, as one web page
per show. Episodes are grouped by season or year under headings, each title
linking straight to the audio, with date, duration, guests, and a summary
where the show gives one. Appendixes list every episode again by date, by
title, and by guest, which a podcast app will not do.
[Download][c-podcasts-zip].

## Homer Directories {#homer-directories}

Each directory saves the reader the search: someone has already visited the
sources, checked the links, and written down what was found. Each is one web
page under headings, in alphabetical order, with appendixes for browsing a
second way.

The web page for all of them is [Homer Directories][p-directories].

### AudioVault {#d-audiovault}

1444 movies and 1471 television seasons and specials that have audio
description, each title linking to its audio download, with year, genres,
running time, and IMDb rating.
[Open][d-audiovault].

### Blind Apps {#d-apps}

96 apps and tools by 39 developers who are blind or have low vision, each
with a one-sentence summary and its platform, technology, and any AI
involved, plus statistics on the whole collection.
[Open][d-apps].

### Blind Authors {#d-authors}

60 writers who are blind or have low vision, and 89 books they wrote on their
own, each available as electronic text or audio.
[Open][d-authors].

### Blind Books {#d-books}

105 books by 60 authors who are blind or have low vision, each with a
one-sentence summary and its category, formats, and year, plus statistics on
the whole collection.
[Open][d-books].

### Blind Creators {#d-creators}

125 people who author books, develop apps, or make presentations, merging
Blind Authors, Blind Developers, and Blind Presenters so that each person
appears once.
[Open][d-creators].

### Blind Developers {#d-developers}

39 software developers who are blind or have low vision, and the software
each has built.
[Open][d-developers].

### Blind Movers {#d-movers}

More than 130 resources for travel, recreation, and tourism while blind or
low vision: the apps, organizations, tours, and services that plan for blind
travelers.
[Open][d-movers].

### Blind Presenters {#d-presenters}

42 people who lead in podcasts, talks, video, broadcast, and film, from 2010
onward.
[Open][d-presenters].

### Tech Training {#d-techtraining}

1304 free video and audio presentations on technology and accessibility, from
25 sources in 20 topic categories, each linking straight to the presentation.
[Open][d-techtraining].

## Homer Tools {#homer-tools}

The apps do different jobs but are built the same way, on the
[HomerDev](#v-homerdev) kit, which gives them consistent behavior and makes
them more reliable:

- 64-bit Windows programs, written in C# or Python, MIT-licensed, with source
  code on GitHub.
- Most run as a dialog or from the command line, with the same options either
  way and named alike across apps, so hand work can be scripted.
- One simple installer each, offered here as a direct download.
- Speech through JAWS and NVDA directly, and a Windows notification for other
  screen readers.
- Work done on your computer, not on a server.

Each ships the same documents, in HTML and Markdown: **ReadMe** for a quick
start, **Announce** for what is new, its **own guide** for full reference,
**Developer** for how it is built, **History** for changes, and **Hotkeys**
for every keystroke.

The web page for all of them is [Homer Tools][p-tools].

### 2htm {#t-2htm}

Converts Word, Excel, PowerPoint, PDF, and Markdown files to clean HTML or
plain text, keeping the headings, lists, tables, and reading order a screen
reader needs.
[Project page][t-2htm]. [Installer][t-2htm-setup].

### bookFido {#t-bookfido}

One catalog of your books across Audible, Bookshare, Goodreads, Kindle, and
NLS BARD, read through your own signed-in Microsoft Edge, with nothing about
your accounts leaving the computer.
[Project page][t-bookfido]. [Installer][t-bookfido-setup].

### DbDo {#t-dbdo}

Manages SQLite, Access, Excel, and other databases from the keyboard, as
Windows lists you move through cell by cell or a dot prompt for commands and
SQL. Template databases start you off -- a job search, a book list, a
collection, a contact list -- and spoken walkthroughs teach the keys.
[Project page][t-dbdo]. [Installer][t-dbdo-setup].

### EdSharp {#t-edsharp}

An editor for text and program code, in use since 2007, that speaks the
result of each command, with snippets, structured text, math, and scripting
add-ins.
[Project page][t-edsharp]. [Installer][t-edsharp-setup].

### extCheck {#t-extcheck}

Checks Word, Excel, PowerPoint, and Markdown files against rules from the
Microsoft Office Accessibility Checker and the Web Content Accessibility
Guidelines, a whole folder at a time.
[Project page][t-extcheck]. [Installer][t-extcheck-setup].

### FileDir {#t-filedir}

Manages files and folders by typed command rather than by mouse, in use since
2006.
[Project page][t-filedir]. [Installer][t-filedir-setup].

### HomerScribe {#t-homerscribe}

Describes video and transcribes audio, returning a copy of the film with
description mixed into its sound. The AI models run on your own machine, so
there is no upload and no fee.
[Project page][t-homerscribe]. [Installer][t-homerscribe-setup].

### HomerView {#t-homerview}

An NVDA add-on, with JAWS support in progress, that asks Microsoft Edge
directly what is on a page: counts of headings and links, accessibility
problems, where a link really goes, and the main article without the clutter.
[Project page][t-homerview]. [Installer][t-homerview-setup].

### urlCheck {#t-urlcheck}

Tests web pages with the axe-core engine and drafts an Accessibility
Conformance Report over all 86 WCAG 2.2 criteria, working through a whole
list unattended.
[Project page][t-urlcheck]. [Installer][t-urlcheck-setup].

### urlFido {#t-urlfido}

Downloads every file of a chosen kind from a web page, driving your own
Microsoft Edge so that sign-ins apply.
[Project page][t-urlfido]. [Installer][t-urlfido-setup].

## Homer Development {#homer-development}

Every Homer Tool is now built on one kit rather than each carrying its own
copy of the same code. That gives the tools consistent dialogs, keys, speech,
settings, logs, installers, and release steps, and makes them more reliable:
a fix made once reaches all of them at the next build. Anyone building a
Windows app for screen reader users is welcome to the same starting point.

### HomerDev {#v-homerdev}

The Homer Development Kit: the shared C# and Python classes the tools are
built on -- dialogs laid out in code rather than by mouse, speech through
JAWS and NVDA, settings files, key names, logging, and paths -- with app
templates, a generator for a new app, scripts that check, tidy, and release
a project, and the conventions written down. There is no installer: it is
source code, kept in one folder and compiled into each app at build time.
[Project page][v-homerdev]. [Download][v-homerdev-zip].
